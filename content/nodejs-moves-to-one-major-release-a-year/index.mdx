Node.js is moving to one major release a year, and the odd/even split is finally gone. From Node 27 onward, every release becomes LTS.

## What changes

Node 26, which landed in April, is the last release under the old model. After that there is one major per year, and every one becomes LTS, so there are no more odd versions that exist mainly to be skipped. The support window stays at thirty months.

## Why

The reasoning is refreshingly unglamorous. Rafael Gonzaga opened the proposal back in July 2025. He pointed out that most organisations skipped odd releases entirely, while a volunteer release team still carried the maintenance load for them. James Snell described the old schedule as a model built for corporate adoption cycles that mattered a decade ago and hadn't been revisited since.

## What it means for your team

For most teams the practical effect is small, which is the nice part. You make fewer upgrade decisions a year, and nobody has to explain to a project manager why we're skipping a version on purpose.

I've been running Node in production for about a decade, and almost every version I've shipped on was even numbered. The project is making official what most of us were already doing.
