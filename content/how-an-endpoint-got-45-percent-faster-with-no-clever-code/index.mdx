An endpoint got about 45 percent faster and I didn't write a single clever line of code. I ran a profiler, and it disagreed with four experienced engineers.

## A week of guessing

The team had been arguing about this endpoint for a week. One person was sure it was the ORM. Another wanted to move the whole thing to a queue. Someone suggested rewriting it in Go, because someone always does.

I ran a profiler for twenty minutes instead.

## What the profile found

It turned up three problems, and nobody had guessed any of them.

A relationship was being loaded in a loop, so one request became sixty one queries. The classic N+1.

A WHERE clause filtered on an unindexed column. That had been fine at ten thousand rows and was not fine at four million.

We also had a cache keyed per user, added six months earlier. Its hit rate was close to nothing, and we were paying the write cost on every request.

## An afternoon of fixes

Fixing all three took an afternoon: eager loading, one index, and deleting the cache. Deleting a cache feels wrong, but one that almost never hits only costs you.

## Why this stuck with me

"Use a profiler" is advice everyone nods at and then ignores. What I keep coming back to is that four experienced engineers each had a confident theory, and all four were wrong. Without the profile we'd have spent a sprint rewriting the wrong thing. Measuring first is boring, and it keeps being correct.
