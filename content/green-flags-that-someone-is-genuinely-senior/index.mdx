Years of experience tell me very little in an interview. The signal I trust most is a candidate who brings up a decision they got wrong without being asked, and explains what it taught them about the next one. Anyone can describe a project that went well. People who have made enough real decisions have made bad ones, and the senior ones have thought about why.

The second one: they ask about on-call before they ask about the tech stack. That tells me they've carried the pager. The stack is the easy part to learn. How a team handles production, incidents and sleep shapes the job far more.
